CPIC vs CRJMC: what is the difference?

CPIC and CRJMC are both name-based criminal record checks that you can apply for online. Which one you need depends on who is asking for it.

What is CPIC?

The Canadian Police Information Centre is the national system police services use to share criminal record information. A CPIC-based check searches it using your name and date of birth.

What is a CRJMC?

A Criminal Record and Judicial Matters Check looks at judicial matters as well as criminal records. It is commonly requested when applying for a security licence in British Columbia. The exact scope of what is reported is set by the requesting body.

How to choose

  • If your employer, licensing body or agency names a check, order exactly that one.
  • If you are applying for a BC security licence, check your licensing instructions first. A CRJMC is commonly requested.
  • If the request just says “criminal record check”, ask which type the organization accepts before you pay.

Both checks are name-based. If you were asked for fingerprints, you need a different check: name-based vs fingerprint-based. See all options and prices on the online criminal record check page.

Common questions

Is CRJMC the same as CPIC?

No. Both are name-based, but a CRJMC also covers judicial matters and is commonly requested for security licensing in British Columbia.

Which is cheaper, CPIC or CRJMC?

CPIC is $45 and CRJMC is $50, in Canadian dollars.

Do I need a CRJMC for a security licence outside BC?

Not necessarily. Requirements differ by province and licensing body, so confirm with the body that issues your licence.
